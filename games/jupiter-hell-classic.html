Quick Overview

After spending my last few games playing mostly warriors, I was finally in the mood to shoot something.

From what I can tell, Jupiter Hell Classic is basically the spiritual successor to DoomRL. Same game, just with different assets, different names... and a different planet. I guess even Mars isn't safe from licensing issues.

There's also Jupiter Hell, which I originally assumed was just the same game with modern graphics. Turns out I was wrong—they're actually quite different. Feels a little strange that there's no ASCII mode, though. We'll see how it goes.

First Death

My first run ended on Europa L6.

I wasn't at full health when I ran into two Medusas at once. A couple of shots later, I was dead.

I probably could've healed myself, but there wasn't enough time. The game sets the pace right away: don't overthink it—just start shooting.

What I Learned After the First Hour

Runs here are fairly long, so expect a bit of grinding along the way.

I picked Medium difficulty, and so far the beginning feels surprisingly forgiving. I even beat the first boss with my very first character. That caught me off guard. I'm used to traditional roguelikes where your first few runs last maybe ten minutes before something eats you. Here I was already thirty minutes in, and everything was still going surprisingly well.

I'm still completely lost when it comes to equipment. I can barely tell which ammo goes with which gun, what I should pick up, and what I can safely ignore. I found a blue armor—does that mean the green one is automatically obsolete?

I'm also missing a "wait" command. Numpad 5 doesn't seem to work, and I couldn't find it anywhere in the controls. So for now my strategy is pretty much classic run-and-gun.

People in the community suggested bumping up the difficulty. Then they laughed and added, "Try beating Medium first."

Fair enough. I'll stick with Medium for now and see how it goes.

What I Learned After the Five Hours

So, waiting (wait) is actually possible and often necessary — that’s good to know. People say that on higher difficulties there’s some penalty for waiting, but Medium is more than enough for me right now.

You can smash the crates scattered around the levels. Sometimes something useful drops out — it’s a nice bonus.

Right now I can reliably beat the first boss, and about half the time I take down the second one too. But after that, the real hell begins.

Once I died stupidly: I walked through acid. The long corridor was full of enemies — by the time I cleared them all, I didn’t have enough health left to make it back.

Another time I completely ran out of ammo. All of it. I finished the last enemies with the grenade launcher, and then got quickly beaten to death in melee.

There are traits related to ammo — they seem very useful in the late game. I’m not sure if the third part is the final one, but it feels like it.

Overall, the first part of the game is introductory, the second starts to turn up the heat, and in the third part the game goes full throttle. Enemies hit hard, tank a lot of damage, and ammo drops are scarce.

I still haven’t really used plasma weapons. I’ve been carrying around 40 energy cells on average — completely useless weight so far.

One useful feature: when all enemies on a level are dead, the level name changes color from red to blue. It really helps with navigation.

What I Learned After the Fifteen Hours

I’ve beaten the game on Hard and even completed one challenge — Angel of Marksman. The pistols-only challenge was a real test.

I tried different classes and lost with all of them. Sometimes very quickly. Of course, I took the dual-pistol traits, which meant I spent most of the time reloading.

Closer to the middle of the game, the 7.62 sidearm appears — it fires two bullets at once and burns through ammo extremely fast.

One of my runs ended exactly like that — I simply ran out of ammo. I threw my remaining grenades at the enemies and that was it. Corpse.

You’d think I should take the ammo-related trait, but it feels like such a waste of points when I’m barely surviving as it is.

Another run ended because a Warlock was resurrecting enemies faster than I could kill them. Only six shots total, the Warlock doesn’t die in one hit, and while I’m reloading both pistols the whole room fills up again.

Overall, the challenges are great — they make you look at the game differently.

I still haven’t figured out how to properly plan builds around specific weapon and armor upgrades. Usually I just pick whatever gives the most immediate benefit, and sometimes the weapon or armor changes its name. For example, tactical armor often comes together by accident.

Sure, you can check which assemblies are unlocked, but planning them is still difficult. There’s no inventory space.

Ultraviolence is waiting for me ahead — once I beat that, I can consider the game completed. Nightmare is too much, I don’t even want to try it. At least not until the game reaches version 1.0.

Now I can push deeper into this shithole.

There’s a section on the base we used to call the Mimir Habitat. Time to see what the fuck is going on in there.
